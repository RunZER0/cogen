# Product contract

## The problem
People often discover decisive facts after committing capital: the location cannot support the required sales volume, the working-capital reserve was too small, a permit has prerequisites they missed, a competitor already owns the catchment, or a supplier/equipment assumption was fantasy. The issue is rarely lack of generic business advice. It is the lack of a persistent person/system with enough time to cross-check the entire decision.

## Product promise
For every venture, maintain a living graph of:

`belief -> evidence -> dependency -> decision -> action -> outcome`

The agent's job is to lower uncertainty before increasing capital at risk.

## State machine

1. **DISCOVER** — collect founder primitives and constraints.
2. **UNDERWRITE** — turn the venture into falsifiable assumptions and a financial model.
3. **ATTACK** — prioritize evidence that can invalidate the model.
4. **OPTIMISE** — mutate only the variables that caused failure, then re-test.
5. **EXECUTE** — convert a surviving venture into registration/compliance/sourcing/launch dependencies.
6. **WATCH** — mutate the model as costs, regulation, competitors, demand or operating data changes.

A venture may move backward. EXECUTE can return to ATTACK when a material assumption becomes weak.

## What should never become a commodity feature
Chat, generic business plans, generic competitor lists and generic checklists are replaceable. The hard product primitives are structured assumptions, provenance, dependency relationships, persistent state, external monitoring, execution state and money-at-risk gates.
